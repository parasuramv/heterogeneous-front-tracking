## Homogenisation findings

For fixed-sign nonvanishing g, the correct coefficient is

$$\bar F(p)=\eta\langle|g|^{-1/2}\rangle^{-2}p^2,\qquad\eta=\mathrm{sgn}(g).$$

The original statement omitted the sign for negative g. It also claimed
period averaging while measuring an anchored primitive; labelled that
quantity with an imprecise negative-Sobolev description; claimed exact period
means for varying prepared data; and used the smooth sine's coefficient
while solving a fixed 16-nodes-per-period interpolant. These are repaired.

The exact map $z'=\rho=1/(m\sqrt{|g|})$, $m=\langle|g|^{-1/2}\rangle$, gives
$u=\rho v\circ z$ and homogeneous flux $\eta v^2/m^2$. It checks the actual
front code through an independent representation. Homogeneous contraction
and BV displacement control yield

$$\|u(t)-\rho\bar u(t)\|_1\le4\|z-\mathrm{id}\|_\infty\mathrm{TV}(p_0).$$

Notebook 08 derives this bound and checks it for each finite-delta prepared
comparison using its common polygonal flux. It does not mistake this for a
bound on the separate finite-delta-to-continuum error.

For 32 nodes per period, delta 0.004, 200 data cells, and time 0.3:

| epsilon | Sampled primitive maximum | Exact stationary-corrector L1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 0.02996214 | 0.02574212 |
| 1/4 | 0.01849459 | 0.01361478 |
| 1/8 | 0.00927315 | 0.00447669 |
| 1/16 | 0.00457153 | 0.00253659 |

The fitted slopes are 0.913 and 1.163. At epsilon 1/16, halving delta from
0.004 to 0.002 changes corrected L1 from 0.00253659 to 0.00253980. Changing
100/200/400 data cells gives 0.00271287/0.00253659/0.00258570. Changing
16/32/64 coefficient nodes per period gives
0.00269817/0.00253659/0.00251132. Doubling primitive samples from 2049 to 4097
leaves the displayed maximum unchanged to roundoff. These are sensitivity
checks, not a universal numerical error certificate.

The smooth effective coefficient is approximately 1.798102. Its 16-node
interpolant gives 1.803632, an error about 0.00553 which does not vanish by
adding more periods at the same microscopic resolution. The 64-node error
is about 0.000351. The stationary counterexample has uncorrected L1 about
0.128544 at every tested epsilon and corrected L1 at roundoff, while its
primitive error halves with epsilon. Thus ordinary strong convergence fails
in general, whereas the stationary corrector removes that oscillation.

## Remaining limits

Finite sampling cannot certify roots of an arbitrary callable. Boundary inflow
is now integrated exactly over front crossings by `flux_through` (resolved
2026-10-14). The sign-changing continuum limit,
the zero-layer limit, and the reported empirical delta/mesh rates are not
proved by this audit. The well-signed quadratic coordinate identity and its
prepared-data corrector estimate have explicit derivations; they do not extend
through vanishing g. Tests strongly constrain the implementation but do not
constitute a formal verification of all floating-point event configurations.
